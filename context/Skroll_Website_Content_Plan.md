# Skroll Website Content Plan

**Direction:** One brand, two arms, emotion over feature-lists. The site should make someone feel something before it explains anything — the way Apple sells a feeling of effortlessness before it mentions specs. "Skroll" itself is a strong asset here: it evokes motion, ease, flow — lean into that as your emotional throughline, not just your name.

---

## Brand Voice Principles

Before any page copy, a few rules to keep every page consistent:

- **Show the feeling, not the function.** Instead of "automated payment verification," say what that *means* for the person — relief, control, no more chasing people down.
- **One idea per line.** Apple-style copy rarely uses full sentences with subordinate clauses. Short declarative lines, lots of white space between them.
- **Let the visual carry the proof.** Your dashboard screenshot, QR ticket, or a clean UI mockup does more convincing than a paragraph of text ever will.
- **The "wow" comes from restraint, not decoration.** Minimalism reads as confidence. Don't fill silence with extra copy — a page with one strong line and white space feels more premium than one with five bullet points.

---

## Site Architecture

```
Home (Skroll — brand + feeling)
 ├── Ticketing (the ticketing platform arm)
 ├── Solutions (business technology solutions arm)
 ├── About (founders, story, why Skroll exists)
 ├── Blog (SEO + credibility engine)
 └── Contact
```

The homepage sells the *feeling* of Skroll as a company. It then splits into two clear paths — visitors self-select into whichever arm is relevant to them.

---

## 1. Homepage

**Purpose:** Make someone feel the brand before they know what you sell. This is the emotional anchor for the whole site.

**Hero headline (tagline, finalized):**
> Skroll. Makes everything better.

**Sub-line (grounds the feeling in what you actually do, still short):**
> We build the systems that make events run themselves, and businesses run better.

**Visual direction:** A single striking visual — could be a subtle looping animation suggesting motion/flow (matches "Skroll"), or a clean split-screen teaser of the two arms without explaining them yet.

**Two-path section (below the fold):**
Two large clickable panels, minimal text, mirroring how Apple splits iPhone vs Mac:

> **For Events**
> Ticketing, reimagined.
> [Explore →]

> **For Business**
> Problems, solved differently.
> [Explore →]

No feature lists here — just enough to make someone curious and click through.

**Tagline placement:** "Skroll. Makes everything better." should anchor the hero, then echo quietly elsewhere — the footer, the browser tab title, maybe as a closing line on the Contact page ("Let's make it better."). Repetition in small doses is what makes a tagline stick without it feeling like a slogan being shouted at you.

**SEO note:** "Skroll. Makes everything better." is low on literal keywords by design (emotional copy rarely is keyword-dense) — that's fine as the H1. Compensate with a keyword-rich meta title/description behind the scenes, e.g. *"Skroll — Event Ticketing Platform & Business Technology Solutions"* even though the visible hero says something more evocative.

---

## 2. Ticketing Arm Page

**Purpose:** Carry the emotional thread from the homepage into something more tangible, still feeling-first.

**Headline:**
> Sell tickets like it's effortless. Because it is.

**Sub-line:**
> Payments, QR entry, and check-ins — running quietly in the background while you focus on the event itself.

**"Wow" moment section:** Instead of a features list, consider a single interactive or visual sequence showing the emotional arc:
> A guest books → a QR code appears instantly → they walk in without stopping.
> Three quiet moments, shown visually (icon or mini-animation per moment), not explained in paragraphs.

**Trust/detail section (placed *after* the emotional hook, for people who scroll further):**
Here you can get more concrete — white-labeled branding, fraud-secure payments, high-traffic handling — but keep it as short accent lines under a small heading like "The quiet details" rather than a formal features grid.

**CTA:** *Bring your event to Skroll* → Contact

---

## 3. Business Solutions Arm Page

**Purpose:** Same emotional register, different feeling — this arm is about transformation, not logistics.

**Headline:**
> Your problem. Our build.

**Sub-line:**
> No templates. No generic fixes. We start with what's actually broken, and build only what solves it.

**"Wow" moment section:**
> A conversation → a problem understood → something built that didn't exist before.
Visually, this could be represented as a simple before/after — a business's manual, cluttered process on one side, a single clean interface on the other. This contrast does more emotional work than describing "automation" ever could.

**Trust/detail section:**
Same pattern — short accent lines: custom-built, problem-first, technical depth, real business grounding (this is where your Moratuwa + corporate/tea-factory background can live as a quiet credibility signal, not a headline).

**CTA:** *Tell us what's broken* → Contact

---

## 4. About Page

**Purpose:** This is where the "wow" gets grounded in real people — visitors who've felt something on the homepage often come here next to see if the humans behind it are credible.

**Suggested shape:**
- One or two lines on *why* Skroll exists (the feeling/mission, not a mission statement in corporate language)
- Founder photos/names with one line each — let the Moratuwa CSE background and business experience (corporate + family business exposure) show up here as texture, not a resume
- A short, honest line about being early: *"We're just getting started — and building in the open."* This can actually work in your favor emotionally; people root for genuine early-stage founders more than polished corporate fronts.

---

## 5. Blog (still needed, even in an emotion-led site)

**Why it matters here:** Emotional homepages intentionally sacrifice keyword density — the blog is where you earn that back for SEO, without compromising the brand feeling elsewhere.

Same content pillars as before, reframed slightly to match tone:
- Problem-led: "The real cost of a no-show," "What manual payment tracking is quietly costing you"
- Founder voice: "Why we started Skroll," "What building in Sri Lanka's startup scene actually looks like"
- Craft/behind-the-scenes: "How we think about solving a business problem before we touch a keyboard"

Keep blog post *titles* more literal/keyword-friendly even if homepage/product copy stays emotional — the blog's job is discovery, not brand feeling.

---

## 6. Contact Page

Keep it minimal and warm, not transactional:
> Tell us what you're building, or what's breaking.
Short form: Name, Email, Which arm (Events / Business / Not sure), Message.

Closing line beneath the form: *Let's make it better.*

---

## SEO Reconciliation Note

Emotional, minimal copy and SEO are in tension by nature — Apple can afford this because they don't need organic search traffic. You do, especially pre-revenue. The way to have both:

- **Visible copy** (headlines, hero lines): stays emotional, brand-led, sparse.
- **Invisible copy** (meta titles, meta descriptions, alt text, structured data): stays keyword-rich and literal.
- **Blog**: fully literal and SEO-driven — this is your organic acquisition engine, separate from brand-feeling pages.
- **Footer**: can carry a small amount of literal, crawlable text (e.g., "Skroll — Event Ticketing Platform and Business Technology Solutions, Sri Lanka") without disrupting the emotional design above it.

This way you get the "wow" experience for visitors and the discoverability you need for organic growth — they don't have to fight each other if you separate where each lives.
